The Books

The Influences Behind Shakespeare's Plays

Unlocking the world behind the words.

Pen & Sword · January 2027

Full description

William Shakespeare is often portrayed as a solitary genius, a fountain of creation sprung from nowhere. This book dismantles that myth, revealing the rich tapestry of ideas, traditions, and controversies that fueled his imagination.

Journey beyond the page to explore the vibrant ecosystem that shaped his work: the bustling world of Renaissance London, from the astrological charts and necromantic texts that informed his plots to the biting satire of the "Poets War" with his rivals; the larger world, from the edges of the Ottoman Empire to the shores of Bermuda, that provided the geopolitical foundations for his settings; the Commedia dell'arte stock characters that inspired his comedies; and the Plutarch lives of the ancients that were the bedrock for his tragedies.

For students, actors, directors, and anyone who has ever wondered where Shakespeare's magic truly came from, this is an indispensable companion.

Who's Who in Shakespeare's History Plays

From Achilles to Anne Boleyn.

Pen & Sword · January 2027

Full description

From the Gates of Troy to the Battle of Bosworth, Shakespeare's history plays are filled with a cast drawn from over three millennia of history, organized chronologically and cross-referenced by play, from Troilus and Cressida and Julius Caesar to the entire cycle of English history plays.

Meet the real Lady Macbeth, a respected queen who made a pilgrimage to Rome; the real Margaret of Anjou, a formidable political operative; and the figures who never appear on stage but whose lives underpin the drama: Edward III, Jacquetta of Luxembourg, and Lady Margaret Beaufort.

Fully illustrated, with detailed genealogies, battle timelines, and comprehensive cross-referencing, this book will transform how you experience these timeless plays.

A Global History of Silver

Five thousand years of power, traced along one unbroken river of metal.

From the first coins of Lydia to the microchips of Silicon Valley, one elemental thread runs through the tapestry of human civilization: silver. This book argues that silver is not merely a form of money or a precious commodity, but the fundamental physical resource that has repeatedly enabled the rise of empires, funded transformative technologies, and dictated the tides of global power.

The flow begins in the ancient springs of Anatolia, where Hittite myths deified silver and Lydian kings forged it into the first coins; it becomes the lifeblood of classical antiquity, funding the Athenian navy that saved Greece, financing Philip II's Macedonian war machine, and arbing Rome's fate, as the Republic conquered the Mediterranean with Iberian silver only to be torn apart by the civil wars this wealth ignited. After Rome, the river moves into the treasuries of the Abbasid Caliphate and along the Silk Road; through the medieval Bullion Famine that stifled Europe and the devastating flood of Spanish silver from Potosí that inflated economies across the globe; and finally into photography, electronics, and the digital revolution.

The book closes in our present moment: the AI boom and the green-energy transition rest upon a new, industrial "silver standard," in which geopolitical dominance may again hinge on control of mines and microchip fabrication plants. It is the same question that drives the Shakespeare books (where does the power on the stage come from?) asked of the whole world.

About

A Shakespeare nerd with a sketchbook.

R. K. Aanstad is a writer, illustrator, historian, and self-confessed Shakespeare nerd with an MFA in theatre. She is the author of Who's Who in Shakespeare's History Plays: From Achilles to Anne Boleyn, The Influences Behind Shakespeare's Plays, A Bawdy Twelfth Night or What You Will Encyclopedia & Dramaturgical Handbook, and A Midsummer Night's Dream Illustrated Handbook and Encyclopedia.

Based in the Pacific Northwest, she co-hosts the podcast Shakespeare and Friends with renowned Shakespeare scholar Darren Freebury-Jones, and has appeared on That Shakespeare Life and The History of European Theatre. Her work lives at the intersection of scholarship and stagecraft: books made for actors, directors, students, and anyone who loves a good story about where good stories come from. Her historian's eye turns next from the Bard's sources to the metal that funded five thousand years of empires.

She shares her home with two bossy cats, two silly little dogs, and a great many books.

Long bio: for programs & press releases

R. K. Aanstad is a writer, illustrator, historian, and Shakespeare nerd with an MFA in theatre. She is the author of Who's Who in Shakespeare's History Plays: From Achilles to Anne Boleyn and The Influences Behind Shakespeare's Plays (Pen & Sword Books, January 2027), as well as A Bawdy Twelfth Night or What You Will Encyclopedia & Dramaturgical Handbook and A Midsummer Night's Dream Illustrated Handbook and Encyclopedia. Her forthcoming A Global History of Silver will be published by McFarland in 2028. Aanstad co-hosts the podcast Shakespeare and Friends with renowned Shakespeare scholar Darren Freebury-Jones, and has appeared on That Shakespeare Life and The History of European Theatre. Her books are built for the people who make and love theatre (students, actors, directors, and history enthusiasts), combining rigorous research with accessible, fully illustrated guides. She is based in the Pacific Northwest, where she shares her home with two bossy cats, two silly little dogs, and lots of books.
